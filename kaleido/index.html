Decimen kaleidoscope · experiment

The same fountain stream, drawn as a spinning mandala of coloured wedges instead of QR codes.

How the symbology works

  • 3 bits per wedge, from an 8-colour palette chosen for maximum separation under a camera's white balance.
  • Rotation is the clock. The innermost ring carries one marker wedge whose angular position is the frame phase, so no frame counter is needed.
  • The outer ring is a calibration strip carrying all 8 palette colours, so the decoder rebuilds its classifier from every single frame rather than trusting a fixed threshold.
  • Nothing below it changed. The fountain, protocol and envelope stack underneath is the same code the QR path uses.

Can a camera actually decode it?

The decoder no longer knows where the symbol is. It finds the mandala by image moments, fits an ellipse, and recovers rotation from the sync marker — so simulated camera below is a real test, not a redraw. Measured with the settings on this page:

  • Square on: yes. 128 KB byte-exact through perspective placement, arbitrary rotation, blur and 4:2:0 chroma. 76 frames corrupted out of ~1050, all absorbed by the fountain.
  • 4:2:0 chroma subsampling is not the blocker. 6/6 frames decode with it on and off at this wedge size — the failure the design notes feared most turns out not to bite here.
  • Tilt is the blocker. 6/6 square on, 2/6 once the far edge is 3% shorter than the near edge, 0/6 beyond that. A barely perceptible tilt kills it.
  • Why: moments fit an affine model, which covers rotation, scale and position exactly. True perspective makes concentric circles non-concentric ellipses, and that assumption breaks. It needs a conic fit and a real homography — the first of the open gaps, not a tuning problem.
  • So: a phone on a tripod, square to the screen, would work today. A phone in your hand would not.

Full measurements, the 4K ceiling maths and the remaining gaps are in docs/KALEIDOSCOPE.md.

Configuration
  • rings × sectors sets the wedge count, and therefore bytes per frame. More wedges means smaller targets for the classifier.
  • sample noise adds ±N per RGB channel at sample time, to probe how much margin the classifier actually has before colours start colliding.
